Does the AI scoring find the right companies?

We spent 37 real Apollo credits (of the 50 allowed) and 126 Firecrawl credits to measure three things: how many companies Apollo has for the client's keywords, whether the AI picks the right ones, and how many leads one search can give. Everything below comes from files saved in this folder.

Result

The AI scoring works. The keywords are the weak part.

  • The AI is accurate. Of the companies it called "good fit", were real plastic makers. It hid 0 real plastic makers out of 64 hidden. Only of companies needed a human decision.
  • Some keywords bring the wrong companies. "blown film", "compounding", "pipe", "masterbatch" and "agricultural film" gave no usable customers in our sample. Machine makers, pharmacies, steel pipes and competitors fill those results.
  • One search gives about 19 leads, not 100. A search reads 100 companies, about 62 are new to the client, about half are good fits, and about two thirds of those have an emailable person. The best keywords give about 30.
  • Deeper pages keep their quality. Pages 2, 5 and 10 gave a similar share of plastic makers to page 1 (5, 5 and 4 of 8). The wizard only ever asks for page 1.

Money spent

Apollo balance went from 4,000 to 3,963, exactly our 37 charged pages. Firecrawl dropped 126 credits for 142 reads we made (failed reads are probably not billed; the client's own enrichment also uses this account, so a small gap is expected). Free calls (People Search, empty pages) were proven free by an unchanged balance.

Answer for the client. One search looks at 100 companies and gives about 19 leads (about 30 on the best keywords). So "100 leads per batch" is too high for a single search. A batch of about 100 companies is realistic. For 200 to 400 leads, run 11 to 21 searches across different keywords or pages.

Two limits make this worse today. (1) The wizard always asks for page 1, so repeating a search finds no new companies. We saw pages 2, 5 and 10 hold the same quality, so a "next 100 companies" option would unlock the pool. (2) Each search can auto-reveal at most 20 contacts (a setting). With about 30 good fits per search, 10 or so would land in "Needs review".

Findings

Recommendations

  1. Fix or remove the keywords that bring the wrong companies: "Thermoformings", "Plastic Injection MoldingS", "blown film" (machine makers), "compounding" (pharmacies), "pipe" (steel), "agricultural film", "masterbatch" (competitors). Replace with product words a processor uses on its site, for example "stretch wrap", "PP woven sacks", "HDPE pipe manufacturer".
  2. Treat cookie-banner, error and placeholder page text as "no text" so the company is read from LinkedIn or flagged, not guessed from its name.
  3. Add a "next 100 companies" (page) option to the wizard. Quality holds on deeper pages.
  4. Raise the automatic-reveal limit per search above 20 when the client wants volume, or accept that about a third of good fits wait in "Needs review".
  5. Keep the cut-offs (0.65 and 0.35). They already give about 91% precision and no missed customers in this sample.
  6. Tell the client: one search is about 100 companies and about 19 to 30 leads, and 200 to 400 leads need several searches.
